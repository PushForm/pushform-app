# Dimensions in Layout

## Place a linear dimension

Choose **Linear Dimension** from the Dimension button's arrow. Click two measurement points, then click a third point to position the dimension line. The tool stays active for another dimension.

Near-horizontal or near-vertical measurements gently align to that axis. A dimension can measure aligned, horizontal, vertical or rotated distance. Its properties let you adjust units, precision, displayed text, font, lines and endpoints.

## Dimension a straight edge quickly

With Linear Dimension active, Ctrl-click a straight path segment, or Cmd-click on macOS, to choose its measurement automatically. Then place the dimension line. Coincident collinear segments can be measured as one chain.

Where measurement points are attached to editable path vertices, later geometry edits update the measurement. Moving the dimension line adjusts its annotation position without moving those vertices.

## Dimension an angle between edges

Choose **Angular Dimension**. Click a straight edge, a different straight edge, then a point to position the arc. The edges need not physically meet; their extended directions establish the vertex.

Click locations choose which side of the intersection is measured, and the arc placement chooses the inside or reflex angle. Parallel or collinear edges do not define an angle; the tool keeps the first edge so you can choose another.

## Dimension an angle using four points

With Angular Dimension, begin by choosing two free points, or two points on the same edge, instead of two distinct edges. These define the first ray. Choose two more points for the second ray, then a fifth point to place the arc.

Follow the status text and preview. The vertex is shown with a V. Escape cancels an unfinished dimension without creating it.

## Change dimension text or placement

Select the dimension and use its properties. Keep the automatic measured value when you want it to update with geometry. A copied dimension pasted through the clipboard uses fixed paper points rather than associations to the original objects.

Check drawing scale and units when a dimension's displayed measurement is unexpected. Editing annotation text is different from resizing the measured geometry.
